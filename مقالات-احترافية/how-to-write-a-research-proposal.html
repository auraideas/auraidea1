How to Write a Clear Research Proposal: Problem, Questions, and Method

A structured guide to writing a research proposal that connects the problem, objectives, literature, method, analysis plan, and timeline.

Aura Ideas · دليل تحريري للطلاب والباحثين

What a proposal must accomplish

A proposal is a decision document. It explains what you will study, why it matters, how you will obtain evidence, and what boundaries will keep the project realistic. Its purpose is not to pretend that results are already known; it is to make the planned reasoning visible.

Build alignment across the sections

SectionQuestion it should answer
ProblemWhat specific gap or challenge requires investigation?
ObjectivesWhat will the study describe, compare, explain, or evaluate?
LiteratureWhat is already known, and where does this study differ?
MethodWho or what will be studied, with which instrument and why?
AnalysisHow will evidence be transformed into defensible findings?
TimelineHow will the work fit the available weeks and resources?

State the problem without inflated claims

Avoid “no studies exist” unless a comprehensive search supports it. Prefer a bounded claim such as “the literature reviewed gives limited attention to this population in this setting.” This is more credible and easier to defend with citations.

Choose the method because of the question

Descriptive questions may need distributions or experiences; comparative questions need defined groups; explanatory questions need a defensible logic linking variables or themes. Do not select mixed methods because it sounds advanced. Use it when combining qualitative and quantitative evidence answers the question better than either alone.

Plan analysis before collection

State how missing data, coding, tests, or thematic categories will be handled. The plan may evolve when you inspect the data, but early planning reduces improvised decisions and improves transparency.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a research proposal be?

Follow your department’s template. Completeness and alignment matter more than a fixed page count.

Should a proposal include findings?

No. It may state expected contribution and outcomes, but actual findings belong in the completed report.
